# Title: Position: AI Safety Should Prioritize the Future of Work

## Keywords

AI Safety, Future of Work, Generative AI, Automation, Job Displacement, Economic Justice, Technical Debt, Shared Prosperity, AI Governance, Copyright, Creative Labor, Collective Licensing, Rent-Seeking, Worker Support, AI Watermarking

## TL;DR

This position paper argues that current AI safety efforts are too narrowly focused on existential risks (like biosecurity and manipulation) and should instead prioritize the long-term, human-centric risks posed by generative AI on the future of work.

The authors identify six main systemic risks:

Increasing technical debt due to rushed AI development and its resulting economic destabilization and job insecurity.

Unchecked automation that outpaces society's ability to adapt, leading to job displacement and skill disparity that disproportionately favors high-skilled workers.

Generative AI firms acting as extractive institutions that concentrate power and wealth, reducing shared prosperity and diminishing worker bargaining power.

Uneven democratization that leaves lower-income countries data-colonized and technologically dependent on a few dominant AI firms.

Impaired learning and knowledge creation due to over-reliance on AI, leading to a call for better AI detectability and mandated watermarking to combat low-quality AI-generated content (or "slop").

The failure of existing copyright laws to safeguard creative labor, as big AI firms exploit protected data under the guise of fair use without offering fair compensation.

The paper recommends a pro-worker framework for global AI governance, including worker support and retraining policies, promoting open AI development (open data, open weights) to reduce the dominance of large corporations, mandating watermarking of AI content, and introducing a fair, royalty-based compensation system (like collective licensing) for creators.

## Abstract

Current efforts in AI safety prioritize filtering
harmful content, preventing manipulation of hu-
man behavior, and eliminating existential risks
in cybersecurity or biosecurity. While pressing,
this narrow focus overlooks critical human-centric
considerations that shape the long-term trajectory
of a society. In this position paper, we identify
the risks of overlooking the impact of AI on the
future of work and recommend comprehensive
transition support towards the evolution of mean-
ingful labor with human agency. Through the
lens of economic theories, we highlight the in-
tertemporal impacts of AI on human livelihood
and the structural changes in labor markets that
exacerbate income inequality. Additionally, the
closed-source approach of major stakeholders in
AI development resembles rent-seeking behavior
through exploiting resources, breeding mediocrity
in creative labor, and monopolizing innovation.
To address this, we argue in favor of a robust
international copyright anatomy supported by im-
plementing collective licensing that ensures fair
compensation mechanisms for using data to train
AI models. We strongly recommend a pro-worker
framework of global AI governance to enhance
shared prosperity and economic justice while re-
ducing technical debt.
