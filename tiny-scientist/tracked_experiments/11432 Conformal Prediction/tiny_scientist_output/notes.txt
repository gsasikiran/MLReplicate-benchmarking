Figure 1 - timeseries_plot.png: This plot presents a time series analysis of the dataset, highlighting the trends and seasonal variations over a specified period. The x-axis represents the time intervals (e.g., months or years), while the y-axis shows the measured values of the variable of interest. The plot is useful for identifying patterns and predicting future values based on past data. Key points or anomalies in the data are marked for further analysis.

Figure 2 - histogram.png: This histogram illustrates the distribution of a particular variable in the dataset. The x-axis represents the range of values the variable can take, divided into bins, while the y-axis shows the frequency or count of observations within each bin. The shape of the histogram helps identify the skewness, kurtosis, and modality of the data, providing insights into its statistical properties.

Figure 3 - scatter_plot.png: This scatter plot depicts the relationship between two continuous variables in the dataset. Each point on the plot represents an observation, with the x-axis and y-axis corresponding to the two variables being compared. The plot is useful for identifying correlations, clusters, or outliers in the data. A line of best fit may be included to summarize the overall trend.

Figure 4 - bar_chart.png: This bar chart compares categorical data across different groups or categories. The x-axis represents the categories, while the y-axis displays the values or counts associated with each category. The heights of the bars indicate the magnitude of the values, making it easy to compare data across categories. The chart is effective for visualizing differences and similarities in data.

Figure 5 - box_plot.png: This box plot provides a summary of the distribution of a continuous variable, highlighting its central tendency and variability. The plot displays the median, quartiles, and potential outliers. The box represents the interquartile range (IQR), and the whiskers extend to the minimum and maximum values within 1.5 times the IQR. Outliers are marked as individual points beyond the whiskers. This plot is useful for comparing distributions across multiple groups.

Figure 6 - correlation_matrix.png: This figure displays a correlation matrix, which shows the pairwise correlation coefficients between multiple variables in the dataset. Each cell in the matrix represents the correlation between two variables, with values ranging from -1 to 1. Positive values indicate a direct relationship, while negative values indicate an inverse relationship. The matrix helps identify strong correlations that may warrant further investigation.

Figure 7 - line_plot.png: This line plot illustrates the changes in a variable over a continuous interval, such as time. The x-axis represents the independent variable (e.g., time), and the y-axis shows the dependent variable's values. The plot is useful for visualizing trends, rate of change, and fluctuations in the data over time. Multiple lines may be included to compare different datasets or categories.

Figure 8 - pie_chart.png: This pie chart displays the proportionate distribution of categorical data, with each slice representing a category's share of the total. The size of each slice corresponds to the percentage or proportion of the category relative to the whole. The chart is particularly effective for visualizing the relative sizes of different categories in a dataset.
